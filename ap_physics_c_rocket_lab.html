AP Physics C Analytical Rocket Dynamics Variable Mass Mechanics & Integration

1. Variable Mass Differential Equation of Motion

Applying Newton's second law for a system with variable mass $m(t)$ ejected at relative exhaust velocity $v_e$:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{F_{\text{thrust}}(t) - \frac{1}{2}\rho v |v| A C_d}{m(t)} - g$$

Where initial mass $m(0) = m_0 = m_{\text{dry}} + m_{\text{motor}}$ decreases linearly during powered flight ($0 \le t \le t_b$): $m(t) = m_0 - \dot{m}t$, with fuel burn rate $\dot{m} = \frac{m_p}{t_b} = \frac{0.0122\text{ kg}}{1.6\text{ s}} = 0.007625\text{ kg/s}$.

2. Ideal Rocket Equation vs Real Numerical Simulation

Assuming zero drag ($C_d = 0$) and constant $g$, the Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation yields burnout velocity $v(t_b)$:

$$v(t_b) = v_e \ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_{\text{burnout}}}\right) - g t_b$$

3. Student AP Lab Guided Questions

Acceleration is given by $a = \frac{F_{\text{thrust}} - F_{\text{drag}}}{m(t)} - g$. Since the engine propellant is being continuously consumed, total mass $m(t)$ reaches its minimum ($51.6\text{ g}$) at burnout $t = 1.6\text{ s}$. Because thrust remains high while mass is minimized, $a(t)$ reaches its maximum peak right before thrust drops to zero.
Quadratic velocity drag ($F_D \propto v^2$) strips a massive portion of kinetic energy during high-speed powered ascent and coasting. While ideal Tsiolkovsky calculations predict apogees exceeding $1,000\text{ m}$, aerodynamic drag limits real apogee to approximately $150\text{--}250\text{ m}$ for an Estes Athena rocket.
The $5$-second delay allows the rocket to coast unpowered to its highest possible point (apogee) where velocity is zero ($v \approx 0$). Deploying the parachute at apogee minimizes structural stress on the parachute canopy and shock cord.
